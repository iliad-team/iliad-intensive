## 1. Prerequisites

- Comfort with probability on finite sets ($\E$, distributions on a finite $\mathcal{X}$).
- One variable of calculus: we differentiate $-t \log t$ once.
- From [Prerequisites (day 0)](/foundations/prerequisites): big-$O$ notation and the idea of a bit.

<LearningOutcomes>

**What**

- Define the entropy of a distribution and compute it by hand for small examples.
- State and prove Gibbs' inequality, and use it to bound entropy.
- Recognise every markup element the LaTeX $\to$ web pipeline supports, and copy this file as the starting point for a sheet.

**Why**

Entropy is the unit of account for everything later in the course that measures information: description length, KL divergence, mutual information. A participant who cannot manipulate it fluently is stuck from day two onwards.

**How**

A morning lecture states the definitions and proves Gibbs' inequality; an exercise session makes the participants reprove it and find the equality case themselves; a reading and discussion block connects it to coding.

</LearningOutcomes>

<Definition id="def-entropy">

**Definition 3.1 (entropy).** The *entropy* of a distribution $p$ on a finite set $\mathcal{X}$ is

$$
H(p) = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}}p(x) \log p(x).
$$

</Definition>

<Theorem id="thm-gibbs">

**Theorem 3.2 (Gibbs' inequality).** For any distributions $p, q$ on $\mathcal{X}$, $-\sum_{x} p(x) \log q(x) \geq H(p)$, with equality iff $p = q$.

</Theorem>

<Solution title="Proof">

By definition, $\KL{p}{q}= \sum_{x} p(x) \log \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\geq 0$ by Jensen's inequality applied to the concave function $\log$; rearranging gives the claim. Equality in Jensen requires $p(x)/q(x)$ constant, i.e. $p = q$. (On the website this proof is collapsible, like a solution.)

</Solution>

<Theorem id="lem-uniform">

**Lemma 3.3.** $H(p) \leq \log |\mathcal{X}|$, with equality iff $p$ is uniform.

</Theorem>

<Solution title="Proof">

Apply [Theorem 3.2](#thm-gibbs) with $q$ uniform.

</Solution>

<div id="exa-coin">
<Callout type="tip">

**Example 3.4.** A fair coin has $H = \log 2$: one bit. A coin with $p(\text{heads}) = 1$ has $H = 0$.

</Callout>
</div>

#### 3.2.2 Exercise session

<TeachingNote title="Intent">

The participants reprove the lecture's inequality with their own hands and find its equality case, so that "entropy is maximised by the uniform distribution" becomes something they derived rather than heard. Pairs; [Exercise 3.2](#ex-gibbs-hard) is the one to discuss on the board.

</TeachingNote>

<Exercise id="ex-warmup">
**Exercise 3.1 (Entropy warm-up).** Let $p$ be a distribution on a finite set $\mathcal{X}$.

<span id="ex-warmup-a">**(a)**</span> Show that $H(p) \geq 0$.

**(b)** For which $p$ is $H(p) = 0$?
</Exercise>


<Exercise id="ex-gibbs-hard">
**Exercise 3.2 (★) (Gibbs, the hard direction).** Prove the equality case of [Theorem 3.2](#thm-gibbs) directly, without citing Jensen's equality condition. [*Note:* this one is solved at the end of the sheet instead of inline, to demonstrate both solution placements.]
</Exercise>

<Hint>

Consider $f(t) = t - 1 - \log t$: it is nonnegative on $t > 0$ and vanishes only at $t = 1$.

</Hint>


<Exercise id="ex-open">
**Exercise 3.3 (Open-ended).** Why might a learning system that compresses its inputs well also predict them well? No solution is given: this is a discussion exercise.
</Exercise>

#### 3.2.3 Reading and discussion

<TeachingNote title="Intent">

Connect the morning's definition to coding, through one well-curated reading with clear take-aways, then a discussion that compares and contrasts. Groups of three to four; steer towards the source-coding theorem if the discussion stalls.

</TeachingNote>

- ([MacKay 2003](#bib-mackay-03), ch. 4), sections 4.1–4.3 (about 40 minutes). Take-away: the entropy is the average length of the best code.
- ([Shannon 1948](#bib-shannon-48)), section 6 only. Take-away: where the axioms for $H$ come from.

<Callout type="note" title="Discussion prompt">

Two participants disagree: one says entropy is a property of the source, the other that it is a property of the observer's beliefs about the source. Who is right, and does [Theorem 3.2](#thm-gibbs) settle it?

</Callout>
