<LearningOutcomes>

**What**

- Define the entropy of a distribution and compute it by hand for small
  examples.
- State and prove Gibbs' inequality, and use it to bound entropy.
- Recognise every component the page supports, and copy this file as the
  starting point for a sheet.

**Why**

Entropy is the unit of account for everything later in the course that
measures information: description length, KL divergence, mutual information.
A participant who cannot manipulate it fluently is stuck from day two onwards.

**How**

A morning lecture states the definitions and proves Gibbs' inequality; an
exercise session makes the participants reprove it and find the equality case
themselves; a reading and discussion block connects it to coding.

</LearningOutcomes>

<Definition id="def-entropy">

**Definition (entropy).** The *entropy* of a distribution $p$ on a finite set
$\mathcal{X}$ is

$$
H(p) = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}} p(x) \log p(x).
$$

</Definition>

<Theorem id="thm-gibbs">

**Theorem (Gibbs' inequality).** For any distributions $p, q$ on
$\mathcal{X}$, $-\sum_x p(x) \log q(x) \geq H(p)$, with equality iff $p = q$.

</Theorem>

<Solution title="Proof">

By definition, $D_{\mathrm{KL}}(p \,\|\, q) = \sum_x p(x) \log \frac{p(x)}{q(x)} \geq 0$
by Jensen's inequality applied to the concave function $\log$; rearranging
gives the claim. Equality in Jensen requires $p(x)/q(x)$ constant, i.e.
$p = q$.

</Solution>

<Theorem id="lem-uniform">

**Lemma.** $H(p) \leq \log |\mathcal{X}|$, with equality iff $p$ is uniform.

</Theorem>

<Solution title="Proof">

Apply [Gibbs' inequality](#thm-gibbs) with $q$ uniform.

</Solution>

<Exercise id="ex-warmup">

**Exercise 1 (Entropy warm-up).** Let $p$ be a distribution on a finite set
$\mathcal{X}$.

**(a)** Show that $H(p) \geq 0$.

**(b)** For which $p$ is $H(p) = 0$?

</Exercise>

<Solution>

For (a): each term $-p(x)\log p(x) \geq 0$ since $p(x) \in [0,1]$. For (b):
$H(p) = 0$ iff every term vanishes iff $p$ is a point mass. [*Hint:* when is
$-t \log t = 0$ on $[0,1]$?]

</Solution>

<Exercise id="ex-gibbs-hard">

**Exercise 2 (★) (Gibbs, the hard direction).** Prove the equality case of
[Gibbs' inequality](#thm-gibbs) directly, without citing Jensen's equality
condition.

</Exercise>

<Hint>

Consider $f(t) = t - 1 - \log t$: it is nonnegative on $t > 0$ and vanishes
only at $t = 1$.

</Hint>

<Solution>

Write $f(t) = t - 1 - \log t$ for $t > 0$ and note $f(t) \geq 0$ with equality
iff $t = 1$. Then with $t_x = q(x)/p(x)$,

$$
-\sum_x p(x) \log q(x) - H(p) = \sum_x p(x) f\!\left(t_x\right) +
   \sum_x \bigl(p(x) - q(x)\bigr) \geq 0,
$$

and equality forces $t_x = 1$ wherever $p(x) > 0$, i.e. $p = q$.

</Solution>

**Exercise 3 (Open-ended).** Why might a learning system that compresses its
inputs well also predict them well? No solution is given: this is a

</Exercise>

#### Reading and discussion

{/* A reading session: what to read, how much of it, and what to take away. */}

- [MacKay (2003)](https://www.inference.org.uk/itila/), sections 4.1–4.3
  (about 40 minutes). Take-away: the entropy is the average length of the
  best code.
- [Shannon (1948)](https://doi.org/10.1002/j.1538-7305.1948.tb01338.x),
  section 6 only. Take-away: where the axioms for $H$ come from.

<Callout type="note" title="Discussion prompt">

Two participants disagree: one says entropy is a property of the source, the
other that it is a property of the observer's beliefs about the source. Who
is right, and does [Gibbs' inequality](#thm-gibbs) settle it?

</Callout>
