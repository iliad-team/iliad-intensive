## 2. Prose, math, and cross-references

Ordinary prose converts verbatim, including *emphasis*, **bold**, `monospace`, and special characters like 50% or §3. Inline math is copied byte-for-byte: $e^{i\pi}+ 1 = 0$, and KaTeX handles comparisons like $x_{<t}$ without trouble. Custom notation defined in the preamble just works: $\KL{p}{q}\geq 0$, $\argmax_{a}\E[R \mid a]$, for $p, q$ distributions on $\R^{n}$.

Display math works as

$$
\sum_{k=1}^{n}k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2},
$$

and as numbered equations you can reference:

<div id="eq-basel">

$$
\sum_{n \geq 1}\frac{1}{n^{2}}= \frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.
$$

</div>

Multi-line derivations use `align`:

$$
\begin{aligned}(a+b)^{2}&= (a+b)(a+b) \\&= a^{2} + 2ab + b^{2}.\end{aligned}
$$

Cross-references resolve to the exact text LaTeX prints: [Equation 1](#eq-basel) sits in [Section 2](#2-prose-math-and-cross-references); below we will meet [Definition 3.1](#def-entropy) and [Theorem 3.2](#thm-gibbs). Lists are fine too:

## 3. Definitions, theorems, and callouts

Label anything you want to reference. Any placement LaTeX binds correctly is valid (top level of the environment); right after the `begin` is clearest.

<Definition id="def-entropy">

**Definition 3.1 (entropy).** The *entropy* of a distribution $p$ on a finite set $\mathcal{X}$ is

$$
H(p) = -\sum_{x \in \mathcal{X}}p(x) \log p(x).
$$

</Definition>

<Theorem id="thm-gibbs">

**Theorem 3.2 (Gibbs' inequality).** For any distributions $p, q$ on $\mathcal{X}$, $-\sum_{x} p(x) \log q(x) \geq H(p)$, with equality iff $p = q$.

</Theorem>

<Solution title="Proof">

By definition, $\KL{p}{q}= \sum_{x} p(x) \log \frac{p(x)}{q(x)}\geq 0$ by Jensen's inequality applied to the concave function $\log$; rearranging gives the claim. Equality in Jensen requires $p(x)/q(x)$ constant, i.e. $p = q$. (On the website this proof is collapsible, like a solution.)

</Solution>

<Theorem id="lem-uniform">

**Lemma 3.3.** $H(p) \leq \log |\mathcal{X}|$, with equality iff $p$ is uniform.

</Theorem>

<Solution title="Proof">

Apply [Theorem 3.2](#thm-gibbs) with $q$ uniform.

</Solution>

## 4. Exercises and solutions

The optional argument of an exercise is its title, exactly as in amsthm. An optional `\important` mark (a $\bigstar$ flagging the sheet's most important exercises) goes right after the label. Subparts are a plain `enumerate`; label an item to reference it. Every solution *names its exercise* — `[ex:label]` is mandatory — so you may place solutions right after each exercise or collect them at the end, as you prefer. Exercises don't require solutions; label any exercise a solution or reference points at.

<Exercise id="ex-warmup">
**Exercise 4.1 (Entropy warm-up).** Let $p$ be a distribution on a finite set $\mathcal{X}$.

**(a)** Show that $H(p) \geq 0$.

**(b)** For which $p$ is $H(p) = 0$?
</Exercise>

<Solution>

For [Exercise 4.1(a)](#ex-warmup): each term $-p(x)\log p(x) \geq 0$ since $p(x) \in [0,1]$. For part (b): $H(p) = 0$ iff every term vanishes iff $p$ is a point mass. [*Hint:* when is $-t \log t = 0$ on $[0,1]$?]

</Solution>

<Exercise id="ex-gibbs-hard">
**Exercise 4.2 (★) (Gibbs, the hard direction).** Prove the equality case of [Theorem 3.2](#thm-gibbs) directly, without citing Jensen's equality condition. [*Note:* this one is solved at the end of the sheet instead of inline, to demonstrate both solution placements.]
</Exercise>

<Solution>

Write $f(t) = t - 1 - \log t$ for $t > 0$ and note $f(t) \geq 0$ with equality iff $t = 1$. Then with $t_{x} = q(x)/p(x)$,

$$
-\sum_{x} p(x) \log q(x) - H(p) = \sum_{x} p(x) f\!\left(t_{x}\right) + \sum_{x} \bigl(p(x) - q(x)\bigr) \geq 0,
$$

and equality forces $t_{x} = 1$ wherever $p(x) > 0$, i.e. $p = q$.

</Solution>

Hints also have a block form: unnumbered, never labeled, rendered right where it is written — a bold lead-in on paper, a collapsible drop-down on the web.

<Hint>

Consider $f(t) = t - 1 - \log t$: it is nonnegative on $t > 0$ and vanishes only at $t = 1$.

</Hint>
